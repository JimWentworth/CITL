THE CRITICAL DISTINCTION

Readable design is not the same as accessibility.

Large type and strong contrast help, but an accessible deck also needs meaningful structure: unique titles, logical reading order, alternative text, usable links, appropriate tables, captions, and content that does not depend on color alone.

COPYABLE SPECIFICATION

Ask for fewer accessibility problems.

The prompt cannot certify the deck, but it can reduce avoidable cleanup.

PASTE BEFORE ADDING YOUR DOCUMENT

Create a [number]-slide presentation for [audience] that helps them [goal]. Use the attached document as the source of truth. Give every slide a unique, descriptive title. Present one main idea per slide in plain language, keep body text concise, avoid text embedded in images, use only visuals that add meaning, do not rely on color alone, and avoid complex tables or decorative animation. End with [desired action or reflection]. Do not invent claims or citations.

USEFUL SHORTCUT

Repair the outline before the layout.

Adobe lets you edit and reorder the outline before it generates the full deck. Resolve missing ideas, weak titles, repetition, and sequence at that stage. It is faster—and more accessible—than repairing dozens of positioned objects later.

OUTLINE GATE

1 One purpose per slide

2 Unique descriptive title

3 Evidence traceable to source

4 Clear sequence for the audience

REVIEW BEFORE SHARING

The checker starts the review. It does not finish it.

Automated checks can flag common failures, but they cannot decide whether the deck accurately represents the source, whether alt text communicates the instructional point, or whether the presentation works for your audience.

01

Accuracy

Compare slide claims, quotations, examples, and emphasis with the source document.

02

Structure

Verify every slide has a unique title and that objects follow a logical reading order.

03

Visual meaning

Add contextual alt text to informative visuals and mark purely decorative objects appropriately.

04

Perception & use

Check contrast, type size, color independence, links, tables, captions, motion, and keyboard/screen-reader behavior.
