THE UDL LAYER

Multiple formats alone do not make a guide UDL.

Use CAST’s three design dimensions to remove barriers and strengthen learner agency.

01

Engagement

Clarify purpose, connect the topic to meaningful contexts, and offer manageable choices in how to begin or persist.

GOALS · RELEVANCE · CHOICE · REFLECTION
02

Representation

Highlight patterns, clarify language, connect prior knowledge, and use complementary media when it improves understanding.

BIG IDEAS · VOCABULARY · EXAMPLES · CONNECTIONS
03

Action & Expression

Build practice with graduated support, multiple tools, actionable feedback, and ways for learners to monitor progress.

PRACTICE · SUPPORT · FEEDBACK · SELF-CHECK

CUSTOM REPORT PROMPT

Design for barriers—not for labels.

Describe the learning context and the purpose of each option. Avoid asking for “a UDL guide” without specifying what that means.

PASTE INTO A CUSTOM REPORT

Create a source-grounded study guide for [learners and topic] that supports [learning goal]. Organize it around big ideas and learning objectives. Include a relevance-building opening and choice of study path; plain-language explanations, vocabulary, examples, non-examples, and connections; retrieval practice, a worked example, self-check questions, and a brief progress reflection. Use only the selected sources, cite key claims, and flag anything the sources do not establish.

USEFUL SHORTCUT

Offer a pathway—not a pile.

Start with one compact core guide. Then add two or three optional formats tied to specific needs. Label what each format is good for so students can choose strategically instead of feeling expected to consume everything.

START HERE

Need the relationships? Open the Mind Map.

Need an explanation? Listen to the Audio Overview.

REVIEW BEFORE SHARING

NotebookLM grounds the content. You design the learning.

A source-based guide can still be inaccessible, overloaded, inaccurate, or misaligned. Test the choices and revise them deliberately.

01

Alignment

Does every section support the stated learning goal and course sources?

02

Access

Are headings, language, links, contrast, and media usable with assistive technology?

03

Choice

Do the options offer meaningful paths without implying that students must use all of them?

04

Accuracy & inclusion

Verify citations, examples, perspectives, terminology, and potential bias.
